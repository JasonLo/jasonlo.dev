The best tools disappear. You do not think about the grid when you flip a light switch. A technology has arrived not when it is on every magazine cover, but when it is boring enough to be invisible, folded so completely into the thing it powers that you only notice the result.

This is how I try to build with AI.

## If I do my job well, you will not know it is there

Here is the principle I keep coming back to:

> If I have woven AI into a system well, you will not notice the AI. You will just notice that the system works.

You will think the search is unusually good, or the summary suspiciously on point, not "ah, a model did that." The moment the seam shows, I have failed at the integration, not succeeded at the marketing.

One exception I quietly enjoy: every so often an observant user pokes at the system, works out what is really going on, and, instead of feeling tricked, is impressed by how cleanly it was folded in. That is the hidden-gem moment. Invisible does not mean secret; it means the craft is there for anyone curious enough to find it.

## People still want the badge, and I don't blame them

I have made this case in design reviews and pitches. The response is consistent and a little deflating: people nod, then ask where the "AI" label goes on the page.

I get it. "AI-powered" sells. It moves investors, press, and procurement committees with a mandate to buy something with AI in it. Invisible infrastructure does not fit in a headline. So the incentive is to point at the machinery and wave, even when that makes the experience worse. It is just how the world works when a technology is still in its loud adolescence.

But adolescence ends. The badge is a phase, not a destination.

## The disappearing act is the goal

Every foundational technology runs the same arc: first magic, then a marketing bullet, then plumbing, finally invisible. Electricity did it. The internet did it. AI will too, and the products that age well will be the ones built by people already designing for the invisible end.

So I will keep hiding it in plain sight. The quiet stuff that simply works is what lasts. That is not AI failing to live up to the hype; that is a tool, finally, disappearing into the thing it was always meant to serve.
